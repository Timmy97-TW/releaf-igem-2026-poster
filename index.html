ReLEAF — Every Farmer a Biomanufacturer

A stress-responsive optogenetic bioreactor for precision plant protection

Crop-protection products exist, but for a smallholder they can stay out of reach. ReLEAF makes the protectant on the farm, only when the crop needs it. A leaf sensor predicts stress, green light switches on engineered Bacillus subtilis sealed inside a benchtop reactor, and a 0.2 µm hollow-fiber membrane lets the protectant out while every cell stays in.

ReLEAF strengthens agricultural resilience by making crop protectants on the farm, on demand, inside a closed bioreactor — shifting from reactive chemical management to proactive, precision crop defence.

Taiwan's climate is swinging faster than farming practice can adapt, and the farms absorbing the swing are small and ageing. We built a benchtop hollow-fiber perfusion bioreactor that grows engineered Bacillus subtilis and physically retains every cell while the protectant it secretes crosses a 0.2 µm membrane into a separate harvest stream. A leaf sensor and a digital twin decide when to produce; green light decides whether the cells produce at all; the farmer dilutes the harvest and sprays it.

The cells never leave the machine, so the thing that reaches the field is a cell-free agricultural input, not an organism. That one design choice — taken on a professor's advice in April, after we had planned a soil release — is what makes the system deployable, and it is what the containment plates test.

Reading crop stress early has always depended on someone who can walk a field and read it. More than half of Taiwan's farmers are over 65, and that skill is leaving with them — just as the window it was built for has shrunk.

Protection exists; access does not. Crop-protection products are on the market, but for a smallholder they depend on cost, availability and getting the right treatment in time. ReLEAF is a filter on that gap, not a new molecule.

ReLEAF started with one question from farmers in Ghana: are abiotic stresses really affecting our crops? Thirty-one stakeholders later, almost nothing of the first design survives.

Four conversations that changed the build

  • A process-engineering professor, 10 April. We had planned to release engineered B. subtilis into soil as a stress-sensing PGPR. He told us to design the hardware as the printer and the cells as the ink. The project stopped being a release and became a contained machine.
  • A plant-engineering researcher, 18 April. We were designing a kill switch. He argued physical containment should be the primary barrier. The 0.2 µm hollow-fiber membrane exists because of this conversation.
  • A protectant chemist, 27 June. We were building production into the reactor before validating the protectant. He reordered the entire experimental programme: test the purified protectant on plants first.
  • A natural-farming farmer, 21 July. “Sometimes, giving too much is not necessarily beneficial for a living organism; instead, it can take away its ability to survive and thrive on its own.” Our thresholds were raised so plants still overcome some stress themselves.

And two that changed the business

  • A cooperative founder, 16 September warned a typhoon “might actually destroy the bioreactor and release the Bacillus subtilis inside.” The reactor moved indoors; only the sensor stays in the field.
  • An extension-station director, 12 October: “Pick one or two specific crops. If you spread out, you are not focused.” Rice was dropped for cherry tomato and strawberry.

Farmer Huiwen Chen, Tamsui, 21 July 2026: “As long as it benefits the plants and does not disrupt the balance of nature, I am very willing to try any new technology. This gives me more time to do what I love most — farming.”

Three cargoes, one claim

  • AcdS (ACC deaminase, Pseudomonas putida UW4) — the cargo every characterised construct carries, and the only one the reactor has produced and assayed.
  • AtLEA14 (At1g01470) and BoPep4, a 23-residue broccoli elicitor peptide — registered, expressed, screened on plants. BoPep4 is the only one with structure modelling: an mRNA folding analysis and a molecular-dynamics study of the wild type, a truncation ladder and the apo receptor at pH 5.5.

All three are claimed as abiotic-stress biostimulants and nothing else. That is a regulatory decision as much as a scientific one — see Safety & rules.

Six blocks — Generate, Restraints, Assembly, Flex, Triage, and codon optimisation — run on BoPEP4, LEA14 and ACC deaminase. Nine molecular-dynamics trajectories, 30 ns each, 270 ns and 25.9 GPU-hours in total.

The result that changed a DNA order

We were going to purify BoPEP4 with a C-terminal 6×His tag. Simulated, it abolished the one contact the peptide depends on — the Asn23 clamp on PEPR1, five contacts holding the C-terminus, not a single salt bridge. The tagged construct passed all four distance-only acceptance criteria while every atom-resolved measure read 0.0%. The tag moved to the N-terminus for every construct ordered after 16 August.

Two designs we withdrew, and one anchor nobody had published

  • K18R, withdrawn. Lys18–Asp348 held in only 53.7% of frames across a 2.55–9.37 Å swing, and a published alanine scan shows position 18 is fully active without it.
  • ACCD F156C was designed against the wrong organism's sequence — caught by alignment before the DNA order went out.
  • Lys7–Asp129, 2.81 Å in 98.7% of frames, is absent from the published hotspot lists. Its loss, not shortening as such, explains why the 9–23 truncation is unstable.

ACC deaminase: modelled, then deliberately left alone

The AlphaFold model of Pseudomonas putida UW4 AcdS (mean pLDDT 97.7) superposed onto the PLP-bound crystal structure at 0.61 Å backbone RMSD over 331 residues, with all ten catalytic residues matching. We then sent no mutant: on an enzyme, the largest available gain is a change to the medium it works in, not to the protein.

None of this has been checked at the bench. No binding assay, no thermal shift, no plant. 30 ns is three to six orders of magnitude short of a dissociation event, so nothing here is an affinity or a free energy. Docking produced 0 high, 3 medium and 26 acceptable CAPRI-graded models and no model within 4 Å of the crystal; an audit found the HADDOCK score 93% explainable by peptide length and net charge alone. The LEA14 interface failed its pre-declared AlphaFold gate (0.16 against controls of 0.85 and 0.78), so no model of that complex was ever published.

A sensor at the canopy reads leaf temperature and leaf wetness, because a leaf is warmer than the air around it and the difference is the signal.

Air temperature and humidity, and the public weather forecast, come in alongside it. The sensor stays outdoors; after a cooperative founder pointed out what a typhoon would do to a reactor in a field, the reactor itself moved indoors.

Bench tested, not field proven. Over 540 paired readings on 10 October the leaf sensor read +1.2 to +1.9 °C warmer than air, and the site means differed (+1.71 ± 0.20, +1.31 ± 0.19, +0.47 ± 0.46 °C) — so one fixed offset cannot correct it. Leaf wetness read 0.0% throughout: the sensor was never wetted, so its response is untested. This is the weakest link in the chain and we say so.

The twin shows what the culture is doing, raises an alarm when a reading drifts, predicts biomass a few hours ahead, and judges from the forecast whether the protectant will be ready when it is needed.

It is a twin, not a model or a shadow, because the decision returns to the machine. The honest limit: so far the models have run on recorded data after each run, and a person makes every decision.

It caught a failing instrument before the instrument knew

An autoencoder trained on the healthy first 120 hours of a 434-hour run flagged the photometer at 216.3 h. The optical density channel failed at 243.0 h — 26.7 hours later.

A forecast registered before the run

On 1 October, before any WB800 run, we registered a predicted growth rate of r = 0.2986 h⁻¹ with a 90% interval of 0.2705–0.3300. The run returned 0.286 ± 0.010 h⁻¹ — inside the interval.

Only the rate passed. Curve shape and time-to-OD-0.5 were not scored and both missed: the prior's trace is 30% off over the first seven hours, and the culture blew through the prior's ceiling before hour 7. A Kalman forecast beat persistence by 41% at 2 h on one run and by 0% at 2 h on another.

When predicted stress approaches the threshold, the app asks the farmer to approve the run. Green light then switches the CcaS/CcaR circuit on; red switches it off. No chemical inducer, no one opening the vessel.

Induction light, built as a 3 m LED strip~535 nm
Off signal~670 nm
CcaSR activation half-time105.1 ± 1.5 min

That half-time matters more than it looks: it makes the control loop dead-time dominated, which is why the twin forecasts hours ahead rather than reacting.

Green does more than red — for three hours

On the light plate, ACC deaminase activity under green light reached 0.1249 mM α-ketobutyrate against 0.0089 mM under red at 13 h — a 14-fold ratio. By 16 h the comparison had inverted. Across the short run, protein stayed flat at 2.6–3.2 µg/mL under both colours while activity rose and green led at every point.

Not a clean dial, and not yet in the reactor. Red did not switch expression off. CcaSR needs about 672 nm to close and our red LEDs emit at 640 nm; well-to-well cross-talk is the other candidate, and we have tested neither. Growth was unaffected by colour, so the difference is not biomass. Every integrated reactor run used ACC deaminase under a constitutive promoter — no reactor number on this poster is the light switch doing anything.

Unlike a batch fermenter the cells are not harvested after each cycle: the culture stays in circulation while product is removed continuously, so one culture supports repeated production.

Three days of protectant, measured

Nine shell-side samples over 72 hours, each harvested by closing the pinch valve for 60 seconds. ACC deaminase stayed above 2 µg/mL throughout, peaking at 6.5 µg/mL at 12 h and settling to 2.5–3.0 µg/mL from 36 h.

Panel C is the one that complicates the harvest argument. Concentration falls after 12 h, but specific activity climbs from under 1 U/mg to 6.3 U/mg at 18 h and holds 5–8.4 U/mg to the end: the later samples are less protein doing proportionally more. We think that lag is post-secretory maturation — cofactor loading and oligomer assembly — but we have not tested it directly.

Both assays are single wells. ACC deaminase fell below the bottom of the ELISA standard curve, so those concentrations are extrapolations, not quantities. In the comparison table a plasmid-free WB800 control read 4.6–5.5 µg/mL — the same range as four of the six constructs beside it — so no difference in that table can be attributed to expression. The assays cannot yet set how long to induce; a person still decides.

The containment result

After 24 hours running, medium was drawn from both sides of the membrane and plated on LB agar. The lumen plate grew colonies. The shell plate grew none, on two separate platings.

A Western blot run on the same system puts the product on both sides of the barrier the cells cannot cross: anti-His bands at 35–48 kDa against a theoretical 41 kDa.

We write the membrane as “designed to retain cells” until retention is validated. These are two platings with no replicates and no recorded detection limit, so they are consistent with retention but establish no containment efficiency. Debris and vesicles of 20–200 nm may still cross alongside the protein.

The farmer collects the protectant from the harvest bottle, dilutes it by the factor the app states, and sprays the crop. No laboratory skills are needed, and no one handles live culture: the cartridge is sealed, dropped in from above, and opens once seated.

Spraying by hand is how the system is used now. Dosing into irrigation needs a pre-mix chamber that is drawn but not built, and the commercial cabinet above is a design: every number on this poster came from the lab rig, a 600 × 900 mm wire-grid panel.

Seven bioreactor runs between June and October, the last four on cells producing ACC deaminase. The machine ended the season holding its own set point unattended for three days.

The seven runs, including the parts we discard

An instrument that cost 3% of the one it replaces

The in-line photometer reads OD600 with no sample drawn. Against a benchtop BioDrop it tracked a mass standard at R² > 0.999 with a median paired difference of 3.8% — so we applied no calibration transform at all, because fitting to the reference would tie our readings to the reference's own error. It cost US$372 against roughly US$15,000.

We had spent three rebuilds blaming our optics. The real cause was the blank: we were blanking against growth medium and the BioDrop is calibrated on distilled water.

All three protectants are secreted

A cofactor control does the same job from the other side: adding PLP raised activity to roughly 39 and 48 nmol α-ketobutyrate mL⁻¹ h⁻¹ for the two signal peptides, against 21 and 32 without it — the enzyme behaves like the enzyme it is meant to be.

On plants

Arabidopsis thaliana Col-0 on agar, hydroponics and soil, under NaCl and 37 °C priming. 100 mM NaCl is the window where rescue is possible: plants show a strong stress response without dying. In soil it took 300 mM.

Conclusion. We have a machine that grows a secreting culture for three days unattended, keeps every cell inside a membrane that still passes the product, and hands a cell-free protectant to a person with no laboratory training. We do not yet have a validated dose.

A poster that only lists what worked is not a result. These are the gaps our own pages declare, and they are the work that follows.

The loop on the left has never been closed end to end. In the wiki's own words: no forecast has yet driven a light, and no light driven by a forecast has reached a culture. In every plant trial the permeate was collected and applied by hand. The six stations are each built and each measured; the chain between them is the work that follows.
  • not shown Light control in the loop. Every integrated run used a constitutive promoter. No reactor number here is the optogenetic switch doing anything.
  • not clean A graded light response. Red did not switch expression off — our red LEDs emit at 640 nm where CcaSR needs about 672 nm, and well-to-well cross-talk is equally plausible. Neither explanation has been tested.
  • failed The AmilCP colour reporter. Despite repeated experiments the medium stayed colourless, and absorbance with and without the construct was indistinguishable.
  • extrapolated ACC deaminase concentrations. All four measurements fell below the standard curve, and a plasmid-free host control read in the same range as four of six constructs.
  • confounded Protectant versus medium. Culture volume was not standardised across groups, so ACC deaminase could not be separated from its medium — and the untreated group sometimes did best, suggesting WB800 culture itself can harm plants at some concentrations.
  • n = 1 Replication. Only two plant runs have a repeat, and each has two trials, not three. Soil fresh weight is one pot per condition.
  • unsettled Timing. Agar and soil do not rank pre-, co- and post-treatment the same way, so no best timing is established.
  • not ours The clearest rescue is trehalose, at 8.7-fold — and trehalose was a commercial reagent used to validate the protocol, never part of the circuit.
  • not built The commercial cabinet, refill cartridges, irrigation dosing, the droplet-leak sensor and the pressure interlock are designs or firmware plans.
  • unsequenced The Level 2 device is verified at three internal junctions by restriction digest, never sequenced end to end.
  • approximate The photometer measures turbidity, not cells. Both calibrations used non-living particles, and no manual samples were drawn alongside the in-line readings. Converting optical density to cell counts would need plate counts we have not run.
  • two stresses Only heat and salinity were tested. Drought and disease are unaddressed experimentally, and BoPep4 co-treatment made damage worse — it inhibits growth even without stress.
  • untested Everything from the modelling. No binding assay, no thermal shift, no plant. Docking establishes molecular recognition only; it does not establish Ca²⁺ influx, ROS burst, MAPK activation or PEPR1–BAK1 phosphotransfer.

Two of these were found by the gates we set before running, not after. That is the point of setting them.

All wet-lab work is BSL-1 on non-pathogenic Bacillus subtilis. Every member retakes the biosafety exam until a full score. Waste is autoclaved at 121 °C for 15–30 min, liquids bleached.

In the reactor the 0.2 µm hollow-fiber membrane is the primary barrier, backed by transmembrane-pressure leak detection, droplet-leak sensing and shell-side turbulence monitoring — the last two designed, not yet fitted. A DURC assessment and restricted access sit alongside. Taiwan's applicable contained-use framework is, by our own reading, still unconfirmed.

Why we call these biostimulants

Ask not what molecule is this but what is the product intended to do. We claim abiotic-stress tolerance only — and park the antifungal application as later work on a different pathway — because that is what decides the regulatory route:

EU — non-microbial plant biostimulantPFC 6(B), 2019/1009
Taiwan — plant growth auxiliary materialsFertilizer Management Act
Review once the file is complete (APHIA)~30 days
Our own estimate to assemble that file1–2 years
If we claimed pesticide activity insteadseveral years

The three pieces are assessed separately: the bioreactor as equipment, the engineered culture as a contained-use GMM, the cell-free output as an agricultural product. Containment is not only an engineering requirement; it determines the exposure pathway regulators must consider.

One finding we did not want: after deeper research into organic regulation we confirmed ReLEAF cannot be applied on certified organic farms.

What we hand to the next team

  • 35 Registry entries built as five swappable modules, so a cargo can be dropped into the registered open cassette.
  • Four open-hardware devices with print files and assembly guides: the perfusion bioreactor, the US$372 in-line OD600 photometer, a US$65.52 dual-wavelength light plate, and a clip-together floating hydroponics plate.
  • An MIT-licensed digital twin on iGEM GitLab.
  • A 15-page standardised Arabidopsis stress protocol, and a twentyfold scale-down of the Penrose–Glick DNPH assay onto a 96-well plate, so ACC deaminase activity can be measured in any chassis's supernatant.
